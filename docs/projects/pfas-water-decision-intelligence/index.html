PROJECT 01 / NATIONAL WATER INTELLIGENCE · EPA UCMR 5

PFAS Drinking Water Decision Intelligence

A national-scale decision intelligence project that converts EPA UCMR 5 drinking-water monitoring data into transparent engineering-review priorities, utility treatment strategy, lifecycle economics, and constrained capital-allocation scenarios.

From nearly two million records to decisions about where deeper review belongs.

The project asks a consulting-style question: which public water systems show the strongest evidence of potential PFOA/PFOS treatment need, what should be evaluated next, and how should limited infrastructure funding be prioritized?

01

Why this project matters

PFAS occurrence data are nationally important but difficult to translate directly into infrastructure decisions. A long table of detections does not tell a utility, engineer, funder, or policy team which systems warrant deeper review first, what treatment choices are credible, or how limited assistance dollars should be allocated.

02

What was developed

The project builds a reproducible decision chain from EPA monitoring records through PFOA/PFOS screening, transparent engineering-priority scoring, verified utility case studies, 20-year lifecycle cost scenarios, and mixed-integer capital-allocation optimization.

03

What it means

The result is not a compliance declaration. It is a planning framework that separates measured evidence from engineering assumptions, verified utility facts, cost assumptions, and policy choices so each decision can be reviewed, challenged, and improved.

04 / DECISION ARCHITECTURE

An auditable chain from monitoring data to capital strategy.

Rather than hiding assumptions inside a notebook, the project separates each decision layer so reviewers can see where measured evidence ends and planning judgment begins.

06 / VERIFIED UTILITY CASE STUDIES

Three systems. Three different decision stages.

The project validates why national ranking is only the first step by examining utilities at implementation, remediation, and pilot/preliminary-engineering stages.

RANK 05 · NORTH CAROLINA

Fayetteville PWC

Large surface-water utility · GAC selected

95.8 priority score

PWC selected granular activated carbon after site-specific evaluation. Published context includes roughly $133M projected GAC upgrade cost, $84M secured funding, a reported $49.6M remaining need, and anticipated GAC O&M of $10–12M per year.

RANK 03 · PENNSYLVANIA

Emmaus Borough

Groundwater utility · remediation / commissioning

99.1 priority score

The Borough’s published alternatives analysis compared treatment, bulk purchase, and hybrid strategies. Treating the wells carried the lowest published 20-year present-worth range at $24M–$30M.

RANK 43 · MINNESOTA

Waite Park

Small groundwater utility · pilot + preliminary engineering

89.4 priority score

A 12–18 month PFAS pilot and preliminary engineering are underway. The project intentionally does not force a final GAC-versus-IX choice before the pilot determines the treatment basis.

07 / LIFECYCLE ECONOMICS

Planning beyond the first construction check.

A 20-year present-value sensitivity model converts published utility capital and O&M ranges into low, base, and high planning scenarios using 2%, 3%, and 5% real discount rates.

FAYETTEVILLE · BASE MIDPOINT · 3%$303.3M

Approximately $139.7M midpoint capital plus $163.7M present value of 20-year O&M. The long-term challenge is increasingly about construction, media and O&M exposure, commissioning, and financing burden.

EMMAUS · BASE MIDPOINT · 3%$28.65M

Approximately $16.75M midpoint capital plus $11.90M O&M present value, which sits inside the Borough’s published $24M–$30M present-worth range for treating all wells.

False precision avoided

Waite Park treatment CAPEX is intentionally left blank because the active pilot is meant to determine the treatment basis. Pilot and preliminary-engineering costs are not treated as final construction estimates.

08 / CAPITAL ALLOCATION

What changes when funding is constrained?

Mixed-integer optimization tests standardized partial-assistance portfolios under different policy objectives and program budgets from $100M to $1B.

Balanced portfolio scenarios

The balanced objective combines priority and a coarse exposure emphasis with minimum small- and large-system representation and a state concentration cap. These are explicit program assumptions, not full construction-cost estimates.

09 / WHERE THIS WORK APPLIES

From occurrence data to infrastructure decisions.

This framework is relevant to drinking-water utilities, engineering consultants, state revolving-fund programs, emerging-contaminant assistance programs, public-sector capital planning, environmental analytics, treatment-alternative screening, affordability analysis, and infrastructure portfolio prioritization.

10 / LIMITATIONS

What the national model does not know.

The current dataset does not provide nationally consistent current design flow, exact current population, project readiness, site-specific treatment cost, or complete current process-train information for every system. UCMR size class is therefore used only as an explicit exposure proxy, and utility-reported treatment/source fields are treated as context rather than verified current engineering conditions.

11 / NEXT ITERATION

What would strengthen the decision model.

Add exact current population served, disadvantaged-community and affordability indicators, project readiness and permitting, secured funding and remaining funding gap, site-specific design flow and treatment cost, state priority-list rules, resilience co-benefits, and utility match capacity.
