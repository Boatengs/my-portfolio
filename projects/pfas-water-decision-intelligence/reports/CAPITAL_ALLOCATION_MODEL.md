# PFAS Capital Assistance Allocation Model

## Executive question
Given a constrained PFAS assistance budget, which screened public water systems should receive partial program assistance first?

## What the model is — and is not
This is a **program allocation sandbox**, not a national construction-cost estimate.

The project does not have nationally consistent design flow, exact current population, current treatment train, media life, civil/site scope or project-readiness data for all 1,127 systems. Instead, each candidate receives a **standardized partial assistance award assumption**. Verified case-study project costs remain separate.

## Budget scenarios
The model tests **$100M, $250M, $500M and $1B**.

## Base standardized awards
| UCMR size | First flagged point | Additional flagged point | Award cap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| S (<10,000) | $2.0M | +$0.5M | $5.0M |
| L (>10,000) | $5.0M | +$1.0M | $15.0M |

These values are explicit **policy assumptions**, not EPA cost equations.

## Policy objectives
1. **Priority Reach** — maximize total engineering-priority points funded.
2. **Exposure Emphasis** — priority score with a coarse `L=2, S=1` size-class multiplier.
3. **Balanced Portfolio** — exposure-emphasis objective plus at least 35% small systems, at least 35% large systems, and no single state above 20% of program budget.

## Balanced portfolio results
| Budget | Systems | Tier 1 | Small share | Large share | States | Priority points |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $100M | 32 | 32 | 62.5% | 37.5% | 13 | 2791 |
| $250M | 81 | 81 | 64.2% | 35.8% | 24 | 6746 |
| $500M | 162 | 141 | 64.8% | 35.2% | 31 | 12895 |
| $1B | 317 | 218 | 64.0% | 36.0% | 36 | 23741 |

## Consulting interpretation
The ranking does not decide the funding portfolio by itself. Award size assumptions, exposure emphasis, small-system policy and geographic concentration rules materially change who is selected. Real-world program use should add exact population, affordability/disadvantaged-community indicators, project readiness, current funding gaps, site-specific treatment cost, state priority-list rules and match capacity.
