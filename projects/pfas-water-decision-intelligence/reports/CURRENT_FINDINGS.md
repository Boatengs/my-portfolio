# PFAS Drinking Water Decision Intelligence — Current Findings

## Project objective

Identify U.S. public water systems with strong evidence of potential PFOA/PFOS treatment need, prioritize where deeper engineering review is warranted, and prepare the analytical foundation for treatment alternatives, cost modeling, capital planning, and utility case studies.

This is a planning and technical-screening project. UCMR 5 occurrence results do **not** by themselves establish regulatory compliance or noncompliance.

## National dataset

- 1,992,002 analytical-result records
- 10,313 public water systems
- 26,545 facilities
- 26,732 sampling points
- 30 monitored contaminants (29 PFAS plus lithium)

Basic structural QA found no malformed collection dates, no missing/unparseable MRL values, no detected records missing numeric concentrations, and no censored records carrying numeric concentrations.

## PFOA / PFOS screening

The screening logic follows EPA's UCMR technical-assistance comparison approach: complete monitoring set required; results below the UCMR MRL treated as zero for the EPA-method location average; PFOA/PFOS comparison uses the 4.05 ng/L threshold for the two-significant-digit comparison above 4.0 ng/L.

Results:
- 1,356 complete sampling-location PFOA averages met the EPA comparison criterion
- 1,456 complete sampling-location PFOS averages met the EPA comparison criterion
- 779 PWSs had at least one PFOA screening trigger
- 875 PWSs had at least one PFOS screening trigger
- 1,127 PWSs had at least one PFOA or PFOS screening trigger
- 527 PWSs had both PFOA and PFOS screening triggers somewhere in the system

These are screening triggers, not violation determinations.

## Engineering-review prioritization

All 1,127 screened PWSs are retained. Current distribution:
- 388 Tier 1 — highest
- 426 Tier 2 — high
- 289 Tier 3 — review
- 24 Tier 4 — watch

The portfolio includes 660 UCMR large systems, 467 small systems, 200 systems reporting awareness of a potential PFAS source, 55 reporting a targeted PFAS treatment modification, and 667 reporting NMT somewhere among triggered points.

## Highest review priorities

1. City of Ocala, Florida
2. City of Downey Water Department, California
3. Emmaus Borough Public Water, Pennsylvania
4. City of North Miami, Florida
5. Fayetteville Public Works Commission, North Carolina

These rankings indicate where deeper utility-specific review is justified; they are not declarations of noncompliance, treatment inadequacy, or health risk.

## Verified utility case studies

- **Fayetteville PWC, NC** — large surface-water system; GAC selected and major implementation underway.
- **Emmaus Borough, PA** — groundwater system with a Pennsylvania PFAS compliance notice; remediation/construction and affordability are central.
- **Waite Park, MN** — small groundwater system; 12–18 month pilot and preliminary engineering are underway.

## Lifecycle cost scenarios

- Fayetteville PWC: base midpoint at 3% real discount rate ≈ **$303.31M lifecycle PV**.
- Emmaus Borough: base midpoint at 3% ≈ **$28.65M lifecycle PV**, inside the Borough's published $24–30M present-worth range for treating all wells.
- Waite Park: treatment CAPEX remains intentionally blank while the active pilot determines the treatment basis.

## Important limitations

Exact current SDWIS population has not been imported nationally; UCMR L/S is a proxy. UCMR treatment/source fields are PWS-reported. ZIP codes are contextual, not exact service areas. National screening cannot replace site-specific monitoring, flow, process-train, design and project-readiness information.
